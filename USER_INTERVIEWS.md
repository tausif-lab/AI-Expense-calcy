# USER_INTERVIEWS.md

## Interview 1 — A.K., Freelance Developer, Solo Stage

He spoke with a freelance developer friend who actively uses AI tools for coding work and personal projects. The conversation lasted around 15 minutes and focused mainly on his current AI subscriptions and whether he would realistically use an AI spend auditing tool.

### Questions & Answers

**Q: Which AI tools are you currently paying for?**
A: “Right now he mainly pays for Claude.”

**Q: Which plan are you currently using and how much do you spend monthly?**
A: “He is using Claude Pro on a monthly plan. It costs him around $20 per month.”

**Q: What do you mainly use the AI tool for?**
A: “Mostly coding. He uses Claude almost all the time for development-related work.”

**Q: Have you reviewed whether the subscription is worth it?**
A: “Yes. He already reviewed it and honestly it feels worth it for him.”

**Q: Have you ever considered cancelling the subscription?**
A: “Yes, but mainly because it’s monthly. He can cancel anytime if he stops using it.”

**Q: What annoys you most about AI pricing?**
A: “The pricing keeps increasing over time once the tools become popular.”

**Q: Do you think you’re overspending on AI tools?**
A: “No, because he uses Claude heavily for almost all his work.”

**Q: Would you trust recommendations from an AI audit tool?**
A: “Probably yes, if the recommendations actually make sense.”

**Q: Would a dedicated AI spend audit product be useful for you?**
A: “Honestly, maybe not. He could probably just ask ChatGPT directly for recommendations.”

### Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising part was that even though he trusted the recommendation quality, he still questioned whether a dedicated product was necessary because general-purpose AI assistants already exist.

### What It Changed About My Design

This conversation made him realize the product must provide structured financial analysis and deterministic recommendations that generic chatbots do not easily provide. It also pushed him to make the audit outputs more data-driven and financially explainable rather than purely AI-generated.


## Interview 2 — Product Manager, Growth-Stage Company

He is a product manager working at  company. He actively uses AI tools for research-heavy workflows, feature planning, and documentation work. The conversation lasted around 15 minutes and focused on how non-technical corporate users manage AI subscriptions and whether they think about AI spending at all.

### Questions & Answers

**Q: Which AI tools is he currently paying for?**
A: “Mainly Claude and ChatGPT.”

**Q: Which plans is he using and how much does he spend monthly?**
A: “For Claude, he is on a yearly plan which roughly comes out to around $17 per month. For ChatGPT, he is using the Plus plan.”

**Q: What does he mainly use these tools for?**
A: “Mostly research work, feature exploration and documentation.”

**Q: Has he reviewed whether these subscriptions are worth the cost?**
A: “Not really. He is usually too busy to properly review all that and just keeps paying for them.”

**Q: What annoys him most about AI pricing?**
A: “The pricing changes too frequently and new pricing models keep getting announced.”

**Q: Did he realize how much he was spending before tracking it?**
A: “Not completely. Recently he started noticing how much he is actually paying for AI tools.”

**Q: Would he trust recommendations generated by an AI audit tool?**
A: “Yes, especially if the recommendations are backed by proper reasoning.”

**Q: Would a dedicated AI spend audit product be useful for him?**
A: “Yes. Since he is busy most of the time, he would rather rely on a structured report than manually analyze everything through ChatGPT.”

### Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising insight was that non-technical corporate users often continue paying for AI subscriptions without actively reviewing their costs simply because they lack time and operational visibility.

### What It Changed About My Design

This conversation made the design focus more on simplicity and report readability. It also reinforced the importance of generating structured, executive-style summaries because busy corporate users are more likely to trust concise reports than manually performing pricing analysis themselves.


## Interview 3 —  Proprietor, Small Manufacturing Business

A conversation was conducted with the proprietor of a brick-making company who uses AI tools mainly for lightweight research, news reading, fashion inspiration, and general information gathering. Unlike the previous interviews, this discussion focused more on consumer-style AI usage rather than technical workflows. The discussion lasted around 10–15 minutes and explored how non-technical users think about AI subscriptions and pricing.

### Questions & Answers

**Q: Which AI tools are currently being used?**
A: “Mostly ChatGPT.”

**Q: Which plan is currently being used?**
A: “Right now only the free plan is being used.”

**Q: What is ChatGPT mainly used for?**
A: “General research, news, fashion inspiration, technology-related searches, and learning new things.”

**Q: Has upgrading to a paid AI subscription been considered?**
A: “Yes, switching to the Plus plan has been considered.”

**Q: What is preventing the upgrade?**
A: “There is concern about spending money on something that may not be fully used.”

**Q: What is most annoying about AI pricing?**
A: “For Indian users, AI tools feel expensive. People here think carefully before paying monthly subscriptions.”

**Q: Would an AI spend audit tool help with decision-making?**
A: “Yes. Before spending money, proper research is preferred to determine whether the subscription is actually worth paying for.”

**Q: Would recommendations from a spending audit product be trusted?**
A: “Yes, especially if there is a clear explanation of where the money is going.”

### Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising insight was how price-sensitive non-technical Indian users are compared to technical users. Even relatively small monthly subscriptions create hesitation if the value is not immediately obvious.

### What It Changed About My Design

This conversation highlighted that the product should not only target startups and engineering teams, but should also communicate value clearly for less technical users. It also emphasized the importance of making pricing explanations simpler and more transparent instead of assuming users already understand AI subscription economics.
